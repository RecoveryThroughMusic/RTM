GET INVOLVED

Find your way into RTM.

There is more than one way to become part of Recovery Through Music. You might be looking for somewhere to make music, supporting someone who could benefit from taking part, interested in working with us, or wanting to help RTM continue to grow.

However you arrive, it starts with a conversation.

JOIN IN

Come as you are.

You don't need to be a musician to take part in RTM. Some people arrive with years of musical experience. Others haven't played since school, or have never picked up an instrument before.

You can start wherever feels right. You might join a song, try an instrument, listen for a while, bring an idea, or simply get to know the room and the people in it.

There is no audition, and nothing you need to prepare.

Come through the door. We'll take it from there.

REFER SOMEONE

Sometimes an introduction is enough.

If you are supporting someone who might enjoy being part of RTM, you can make an introduction or referral.

You don't need to decide whether someone is musical enough, or whether RTM will be the right fit. Tell us a little about the person, what has brought you to RTM, and anything that might help us make their first contact easier.

Where possible, we want the person being referred to know about RTM and have a say in taking the next step.

ABOUT YOU

WORK WITH RTM

Let's make something that fits.

RTM works with organisations and communities interested in creating meaningful opportunities for people to make music, participate and connect.

That might be a one-off workshop, a series of sessions or a longer collaboration. Rather than bringing in a fixed programme, we begin with a conversation about the people, the setting and what you hope to make possible.

Our approach has developed through community recovery work and has also travelled into prisons and work with asylum seekers and refugees.

If you have an idea — even if it isn't fully formed yet — we'd be interested to hear about it.

SUPPORT RTM

Help make room for more music.

RTM has grown through people and organisations who have believed in the work and found different ways to support it.

You might want to make a donation, offer equipment or resources, support a particular piece of work, or introduce us to someone who might be able to help.

Every contribution can help create more opportunities for people to come together, make music and belong.
